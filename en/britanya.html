Battle of Britain

The first major battle in history fought entirely by air forces. Germany's plan to invade Britain was defeated in the skies over England.

01 Background

After France surrendered in June 1940, Britain was left as the only major power in Europe still fighting Germany.

Hitler planned a seaborne invasion codenamed "Sea Lion". To cross the English Channel safely, however, Germany first had to put Britain's Royal Air Force (RAF) out of action.

02 The battle

The Luftwaffe first targeted shipping convoys and ports in the Channel, and from August onwards the RAF's airfields and radar stations.

Thanks to a chain of radar stations along the coast and a central control system, the British could see attacks coming and send their Spitfires and Hurricanes to the right place.

In early September Germany switched its attacks to London. The bombing of cities took a heavy toll on civilians, but it also gave the RAF time to recover. The defeat of a major German raid on 15 September is still marked today as "Battle of Britain Day".

03 Outcome and significance

The Luftwaffe failed to win air superiority, and in mid-September Hitler postponed the invasion indefinitely. For the first time in the war, Germany had failed to achieve one of its goals.

Britain staying in the war also meant that the base needed for the later Normandy landings was preserved.
