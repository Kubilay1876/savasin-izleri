Fronts / Normandy

WESTERN EUROPE

Normandy Landings

Also known as D-Day, the largest seaborne invasion in history. With this operation the Allies opened a second front in Western Europe.

01 Background

For a long time the Soviet Union had been asking the Allies to open a second front in the West to ease the pressure on the Eastern Front.

Germany had built a line of coastal defences along France's shores known as the "Atlantic Wall". The Allies carried out an elaborate deception plan to convince the Germans that the landing would take place further north, at Calais.

02 The battle

On the night of 5–6 June, thousands of paratroopers were dropped behind the landing area. In the morning, landings were made on five separate beaches (Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno and Sword); that day around 156,000 troops reached France by sea and air.

The heaviest losses were at Omaha Beach, where American troops met intense fire beneath steep bluffs. The photograph on the site's home page is "Into the Jaws of Death", taken by Robert F. Sargent at Omaha that morning.

03 Outcome and significance

By the end of the day the Allies had a foothold on the coast, and they went on to fight a battle in Normandy that lasted about two months. Paris was liberated on 25 August.

Germany was now being squeezed at the same time by the Soviets in the east and by American, British and Canadian armies in the west.
