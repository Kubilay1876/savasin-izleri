THE END IN EUROPE

Fall of Berlin

The last major battle of the war in Europe. When Soviet armies captured the capital of Nazi Germany, the war in Europe came to an end.

01 Background

By early 1945 the Red Army had crossed Poland and reached the Oder River, about 70 kilometres from Berlin. In the west, American and British armies had crossed the Rhine.

Hitler and part of the Nazi leadership chose to stay in the bunker beneath the Reich Chancellery in Berlin.

02 The battle

On 16 April Soviet armies launched a massive offensive from the Oder and from the south. The German defences on the Seelow Heights were broken at a heavy cost, and within a few days the city was surrounded.

Alongside regular troops, the city's defenders included militia units made up of older men and boys from the Hitler Youth. During the fighting, most of the civilians still in the city tried to survive in cellars and shelters.

03 Outcome and significance

Hitler took his own life in the bunker on 30 April; on the same day Soviet soldiers reached the Reichstag. The Berlin garrison surrendered on 2 May, and Germany's unconditional surrender came into force on 8 May.

After the war the city was divided into four occupation zones. This division made Berlin a symbol of the tension between East and West throughout the Cold War.
