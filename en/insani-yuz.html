04 / THE HUMAN COST OF WAR

Beyond the maps, human lives.

The war was not fought only on the front lines. Most of the 70–85 million people who lost their lives were civilians. Bombing, forced migration, hunger and persecution changed the lives of millions of families forever.

Understanding the past also means remembering its human consequences.

THE CIVILIANS' WAR

The people who lived it.

01

Cities under bombs

Air raids brought the war from the front lines into the heart of cities. Tens of thousands of civilians were killed in attacks on cities such as London, Rotterdam, Hamburg, Dresden and Tokyo, and people spent their nights in shelters and underground stations.

02

The Holocaust

The Nazi regime set out to systematically destroy the Jews of Europe. Around six million Jews, along with hundreds of thousands of Roma, disabled people and members of other targeted groups, were murdered in ghettos, concentration camps and extermination camps. 27 January, the day Auschwitz was liberated, is now International Holocaust Remembrance Day.

03

Refugees and displacement

The war drove millions of people from their homes. Those fleeing bombing, occupation and persecution, those taken away for forced labour, and those who lost their homeland as borders changed after the war were scattered across Europe.

04

Life on the home front

As men went to the front, women worked in factories, on farms and in hospitals. Food, fuel and clothing were rationed, and in Britain children were evacuated from the big cities to the countryside to protect them from bombing.

TURKEY DURING THE WAR1939 — 1945

NEUTRAL BUT AFFECTED

Outside the war, within its reach.

Turkey remained neutral for most of the war, yet the war affected the country deeply. Hundreds of thousands of men were kept under arms in case of an attack, and the labour drawn away from fields and factories reduced production.

The National Protection Law of January 1940 placed prices and production under state control. In January 1942, bread began to be rationed in Istanbul and Ankara.

The Capital Tax (Varlık Vergisi), introduced in November 1942, was applied far more heavily to non-Muslim citizens; those who could not pay had their property seized or were sent to labour camps in Aşkale.

To take part in the founding conference of the United Nations, Turkey symbolically declared war on Germany and Japan on 23 February 1945.
