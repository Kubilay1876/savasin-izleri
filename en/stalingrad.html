Fronts / Stalingrad

EASTERN FRONT

Battle of Stalingrad

One of the bloodiest battles of the war. Street-by-street fighting in the city on the Volga ended with the complete destruction of a German army.

01 Background

In the summer of 1942, Germany launched a major offensive in the south to capture the oil fields of the Caucasus.

Stalingrad, on the banks of the Volga, was an important industrial and transport centre, and because it bore Stalin's name it had great symbolic value for both sides.

02 The battle

The attacks, which began with heavy air raids in August 1942, left much of the city in ruins. The battle soon turned into urban warfare fought street by street and building by building, with the Soviet 62nd Army holding a narrow strip along the Volga.

On 19 November the Soviets launched "Operation Uranus", attacking from the north and south of the city and encircling the German Sixth Army.

Attempts to supply the trapped troops by air and to break the encirclement from outside failed. In the freezing winter, hunger and disease drove losses up quickly.

03 Outcome and significance

The remaining forces of the Sixth Army surrendered between 31 January and 2 February 1943, and about 91,000 German soldiers were taken prisoner. Total losses on both sides (killed, wounded and captured) are estimated at around two million.

After Stalingrad the advantage on the Eastern Front passed to the Soviets, and the German army never again advanced so far east.
